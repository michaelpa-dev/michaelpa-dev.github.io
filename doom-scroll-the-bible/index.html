Privacy Policy

Effective September 15, 2026 · Michael Lehotsky

Doom Scroll the Bible works without an account. Your reading history and saved content stay on this device unless you choose to copy, share, export, or contact support. The app contacts Expo to check for and download app updates. Expo also uses those requests to provide aggregate app-usage information. The app has no advertising and does not sell your personal information or track you across other companies’ apps.

1. Reading without an account

Doom Scroll the Bible is an offline-first Bible reader. You do not create an account to use it. The Bible text and app fonts are bundled with the app, so ordinary reading does not send a reading history, notes, or saved passages to a server operated by the developer.

The app does not include advertising or a developer-operated cloud sync service. We do not sell personal information or use information from the app to track you across other companies’ apps or websites. Expo’s limited app-usage measurement from update requests is described in App updates below.

2. Information stored on your device

The app stores your current passage, reading mode, scroll position, return place, bookmarks, highlights, notes, unfinished note drafts, completed reading guides, welcome-screen choice, feedback draft, local diagnostics preference, theme, font, light or night mode, motion, and other display preferences locally on your device. It also keeps one local pre-restore recovery copy when you restore a backup.

Your notes may contain personal or religious reflections. They remain in the app’s local storage unless you choose to export them or otherwise send them elsewhere. The app does not request your precise location, contacts, microphone, or camera.

3. Copy, share, export, and restore

When you copy or share a passage, the app sends the selected passage text, reference, and edition label to the operating system’s clipboard or share sheet. Notes and drafts are not included. Your operating system may synchronize clipboard content between devices depending on your settings.

A backup file contains your notes, drafts, saved references, reading places, guide completion, feedback draft, and preferences. It is readable JSON and is not protected by an app-specific password or encryption. Choose a trusted destination. Any cloud provider, app, person, or service you select handles the file under its own practices. The developer does not receive a backup unless you deliberately send it.

Restore reads the backup file you select and, after confirmation, replaces the app’s local reader data. Before replacement, the app preserves one local recovery copy of the previous data.

4. Retention and deletion

Local app data remains on your device until you change or remove it. Deleting an active note or saved item does not remove copies that may already exist in an earlier recovery file, an exported backup, a device backup, or a recipient’s copy.

Your data → Reset all local data removes the app’s active reader data, preferences, feedback draft, local diagnostic events, and pre-restore recovery copy. Temporary app-created backup files are also cleaned up. A file selected for import may be cached by the operating system. Separately remove exported files or copies held by other apps and services.

On Android, clearing the app’s storage or deleting the app removes its local app data. On iPhone or iPad, offloading may retain documents and data; choose Delete App to remove local app data. You must separately delete exported files, cloud or device backups, and copies you shared with other people or services.

Because there is no developer-operated cloud sync, the developer cannot remotely recover or delete information stored only on your device.

5. Protecting your information

The app relies on the protections provided by your device and operating system for its local storage. The app does not add a separate password or encryption layer to its local database or exported backup files. Protect access to your device and store exports only in places you trust.

6. External links, support, and beta testing

Publisher or source links opened from the app use your web browser. The website you visit may receive ordinary connection information such as an IP address and browser details under that website’s own privacy policy.

Feedback is optional. A draft stays on this device until you choose Preview, then open your email app and send it yourself. The message includes what you wrote, the selected feedback category, app version, and operating system. Reading history, notes, and backup files are not attached automatically. If you enable local diagnostics, the app keeps at most 50 event codes and timestamps in memory for the current session; disabling the option clears them. You separately choose whether to include those events in the previewed feedback. No local diagnostic log is sent automatically. This local option is separate from the update-service requests described below. We receive your email address and anything you choose to send. We retain support conversations only as reasonably needed to respond, maintain the app, and meet legal obligations. You may request deletion, subject to records we must retain.

Apple and Google operate their respective app stores and beta programs under their own terms and privacy policies. When you use TestFlight, Apple may make tester information, usage or crash information, feedback, and screenshots available to the developer.

7. App updates

Reading works offline. Once the welcome screen is complete, the app may check Expo’s EAS Update service when launched; you can also request a check from About or Help. Update checks and downloads require an internet connection and may use mobile data. An available update waits for you to choose Restart. Native app updates are delivered by Apple or Google through their stores.

Expo receives technical information needed to deliver compatible updates, including your connection IP address, operating system/platform, app runtime version, update identifiers, and a randomly generated identifier stored for this app installation. The installation identifier is reused on subsequent update requests; it is not your advertising ID. The update client may also report technical information about failed app updates, including a previous fatal update error.

Expo aggregates update-check requests into app-usage information over time and by platform, which is available to the developer. Automatic update requests and this limited usage measurement are separate from the optional local diagnostics setting. These requests do not intentionally include your reading history, current passage, notes, bookmarks, feedback draft, or reading-guide completion. The app does not add a separate user analytics or crash-reporting SDK.

Expo processes update-service information under its own privacy practices: https://expo.dev/privacy. Apple and Google separately process store downloads, updates, and beta-program information under their policies.

8. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when the app or its data practices change. The effective date shows when the current version took effect. Features such as accounts, online synchronization, advertising, additional analytics, subscriptions, or new developer-operated services will receive a fresh privacy review before release.
